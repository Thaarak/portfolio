Thaarak Sriram

I study data science and physics at UC San Diego, and I do research in two labs. At the Climate Analytics Lab I work with Duncan Watson-Parris on marine cloud brightening: teaching a planner where to brighten ocean clouds by taking gradients through a differentiable climate model. At the Data Science Teaching and Learning Lab I study what happens when instructors hand their course materials to AI coding agents. Our paper on CourseLens, a tool for reviewing what the agents changed, will appear at SIGCSE 2027.

The question under both is the same one: how do you check that a model, or an AI system, is doing what you think it is? More on the research page.

I also like building things fast with friends. Morro, a simulator for cloud-seeding interventions, won the sustainability track at TreeHacks 2026, and OceanOps, a planning tool for ocean carbon removal, won the ASUS Challenge at LA Hacks. The rest are on the projects page.

Before college, most of my time went to climate work in Livermore, California, my hometown. A group of us organized the city’s first Youth Climate Summit, mapped its heat islands with sensors we built, and wrote a climate literacy resolution that the school district adopted in 2024. Some of that made the local papers.

Away from a keyboard I follow Royal Challengers Bangalore, drink more coconut water than seems reasonable, and will argue for Chicago-style pizza. If you want to talk about climate models, AI evaluation or cricket, write to me.
